Weight Loss Plateau: Why It Happens and How to Break It

Most plateaus aren't plateaus. Here's how to tell, and what to do when it really is one.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Water weight can hide fat loss for one to two weeks. Judge a plateau after 2 to 3 weeks of a flat weekly average.
  • As you lose weight, you burn fewer calories. Your old deficit gets smaller over time.
  • The most common cause of a plateau is that the plan has quietly drifted: bigger portions, more snacks, fewer steps.
  • A diet break at maintenance can help with long diets (Byrne et al., 2018).

Is it really a plateau?

Daily weight swings by 1 to 2 kg because of salt, carbs, water, digestion and hormonal cycles. A week with salty food, poor sleep or a hard training block can hold extra water that masks real fat loss. It's common to see the scale stay flat for a week or two and then drop suddenly.

Check this first:

  • Look at your weekly average, not single weigh-ins.
  • Compare at least two to three weeks.
  • Check other signs: are your clothes looser, your waist smaller, your lifts holding steady?

If the weekly average hasn't moved for two to three weeks, it's a real plateau.

Why plateaus happen

You need less energy. A smaller body burns fewer calories at rest and during movement. A deficit that was 500 kcal at the start might be 250 kcal now.

Your body adapts a bit. Beyond the smaller body size, energy expenditure can drop slightly more than expected during dieting, partly because people unconsciously move less (fewer steps, less fidgeting).

The plan drifts. This is the biggest one. Portions grow, oil goes unmeasured, a few snacks sneak in, weekends get looser. A review on weight maintenance notes that the typical weight loss plateau after several months is driven largely by declining adherence along with the body's adaptations (Hall & Kahan, 2018).

How to break a plateau

1. Audit a normal week. Track honestly for a few days, including oils, sauces, drinks and weekends. Most people find a few hundred calories they didn't count.

2. Reduce calories a little. Lower your intake by about 100 to 200 kcal per day, mostly from carbs or fat. Keep protein high.

3. Move more in daily life. Steps are an easy lever. Many people walk less when dieting without noticing. A daily step goal of around 8,000 is a good target for most adults (Paluch et al., 2022).

4. Keep lifting. Strength training helps you keep muscle, which keeps your body looking better even when the scale is slow.

5. Sleep enough. Short sleep increases hunger and makes it harder to stick to your plan.

6. Take a diet break. If you've been dieting for 12 weeks or more, two to four weeks at maintenance can reset hunger and motivation. In the MATADOR study, men who alternated two weeks of dieting with two weeks at maintenance lost more fat and kept more of it off than men who dieted continuously (Byrne et al., 2018).

What not to do

  • Don't slash calories. A sudden big cut increases hunger and makes the diet harder to stick to.
  • Don't add hours of cardio overnight. Add movement gradually.
  • Don't quit because of one flat week. A flat week is normal. Patience is part of the plan.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a weight loss plateau last?

A water-weight stall often lasts one to two weeks and then breaks with a sudden drop. A true plateau lasts until you adjust your calories, activity or both.

Why am I not losing weight even though I'm in a deficit?

Often the deficit is smaller than you think because of untracked food, larger portions or less daily movement. Water retention can also hide fat loss for a while.

Do diet breaks really work?

Research is promising. In one study, alternating dieting with maintenance periods improved fat loss efficiency. At minimum, a break helps with hunger and motivation during long diets.

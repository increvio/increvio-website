Macros Explained: How to Set Protein, Carbs and Fat

Calories decide your weight. Macros decide what that weight is made of. Here's the simple order to set them.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Macros (macronutrients) are protein, carbohydrates and fat. Each provides calories: protein and carbs about 4 kcal/g, fat about 9 kcal/g.
  • Set them in this order: protein first, then fat, then carbs fill the rest.
  • Protein: about 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg (Morton et al., 2018). Fat: around 20 to 35% of calories, not much below 20%.
  • Exact macro ratios matter much less than total calories and protein.

What are macros?

MacroCalories per gramMain jobSources
Proteinabout 4Builds and repairs muscle, keeps you fullMeat, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, tofu
Carbohydratesabout 4Main fuel for hard trainingRice, potatoes, oats, bread, fruit
Fatabout 9Hormones, cell function, vitamin absorptionOils, nuts, avocado, fatty fish, cheese

Alcohol also has calories (about 7 kcal/g) but isn't a nutrient you need.

Step 1: Set protein

Protein matters most for body composition. A meta-analysis found that about 1.6 g/kg captured most of the muscle-building benefit, with up to 2.2 g/kg as a sensible upper target (Morton et al., 2018). In a calorie deficit, aim higher to protect muscle.

GoalProtein
Build muscleabout 2.0 g/kg
Maintainabout 1.8 g/kg
Lose fatabout 1.8 to 2.2 g/kg

With a lot of body fat to lose, calculate from a healthy goal weight instead. See How much protein per day.

Step 2: Set fat

Fat is essential, but you don't need a lot of it. A common recommendation is 20 to 35% of total calories. Going much lower can reduce testosterone in men (Whittaker & Wu, 2021) and makes meals less satisfying.

A practical target: about 30% of calories, or roughly 0.6 to 1.5 g per kg of body weight.

Step 3: Carbs fill the rest

Whatever calories remain go to carbohydrates. Carbs fuel hard training, especially high-volume strength training. When building muscle, at least 3 to 5 g/kg is a reasonable minimum.

Example: building muscle

80 kg man, target 2,900 kcal:

  1. Protein: 2.0 × 80 = 160 g → 640 kcal
  2. Fat: 30% of 2,900 = 870 kcal → 97 g
  3. Carbs: 2,900 − 640 − 870 = 1,390 kcal → 348 g

Example: losing fat

65 kg woman, target 1,650 kcal:

  1. Protein: 1.8 × 65 = 117 g → 468 kcal
  2. Fat: 30% of 1,650 = 495 kcal → 55 g
  3. Carbs: 1,650 − 468 − 495 = 687 kcal → 172 g

Low carb or low fat?

For fat loss, studies comparing diets with very different carb and fat ratios find similar results when calories and protein are matched and people stick to them (Dansinger et al., 2005). Choose the split that you enjoy and that supports your training. If you lift hard, you'll usually feel and perform better with a decent amount of carbs.

Do you need to hit macros exactly?

No. Aim for:

  • Calories within about 100 kcal of your target on most days
  • Protein close to target, every day
  • Carbs and fat roughly right; they can trade places day to day

Frequently asked questions

What are the best macros for weight loss?

High protein (about 1.8 to 2.2 g/kg), moderate fat (around 30% of calories), and carbs filling the rest of a modest calorie deficit. Exact ratios matter less than calories and protein.

Do I need to count macros?

Not necessarily. Many people do well with a meal plan built to their targets or simple habits like protein at every meal. Counting is helpful while learning portion sizes.

Is fiber a macro?

Fiber is a type of carbohydrate. It's not counted separately in most macro plans, but aiming for plenty of fiber from vegetables, fruit, legumes and whole grains is a good idea.

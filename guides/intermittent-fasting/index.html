Does Intermittent Fasting Work? What the Research Says

Fasting isn't magic, but it can be a useful tool. Here's what the best trials found.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Intermittent fasting (IF) works through a calorie deficit, like any other diet.
  • In a 12-month trial, time-restricted eating plus calorie restriction led to similar weight loss as calorie restriction alone (Liu et al., 2022).
  • A 12-week trial of 16:8 without calorie guidance found no meaningful extra weight loss compared to a control group (Lowe et al., 2020).
  • IF can be a good tool if it makes eating less easier for you. If it makes you overeat later, skip it.

What is intermittent fasting?

IF is an eating pattern that cycles between eating and fasting. Common versions:

MethodHow it works
16:8 (time-restricted eating)Eat within an 8-hour window, for example 12 pm to 8 pm
14:10Eat within a 10-hour window
5:2Eat normally 5 days, about 500 to 600 kcal on 2 days
Alternate-day fastingVery low intake every other day

What the research says

Liu et al., 2022 (NEJM): 139 adults with obesity were assigned to calorie restriction with or without time-restricted eating (8 am to 4 pm) for 12 months. Both groups had the same calorie targets. Weight loss was similar in both groups. The eating window added no measurable extra benefit.

Lowe et al., 2020 (JAMA Internal Medicine): 116 adults did 16:8 or ate three meals a day, without specific calorie goals, for 12 weeks. The 16:8 group lost only slightly more weight, and the difference wasn't significant. The study also found some signs of more lean mass loss in the fasting group.

Conclusion: IF works when it helps you eat fewer calories. It doesn't have special fat-burning powers beyond that.

Who might like IF

  • People who aren't hungry in the morning anyway
  • People who prefer fewer, bigger meals
  • People who tend to snack late at night (an eating window can stop that)

Who might not

  • People who get very hungry and overeat when the window opens
  • People who train in the morning and feel weak without food
  • Anyone with a history of disordered eating (talk to a professional first)
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding people, and people with diabetes or other conditions without medical guidance

IF and muscle building

Muscle building benefits from protein spread over several meals (about 0.4 g/kg per meal, three to four times a day) (Schoenfeld & Aragon, 2018). An 8-hour window can still fit three protein-rich meals. Very short windows (one meal a day) make that harder.

Tips if you lift and fast:

  • Fit at least three protein servings into your window.
  • Train near or inside your eating window if possible.
  • Keep total protein around 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg.

Frequently asked questions

Is 16:8 fasting effective for weight loss?

It can be, if it helps you eat fewer calories. Studies show similar results to regular calorie restriction when calories are matched.

Does intermittent fasting burn more fat?

Not beyond what the calorie deficit explains, according to the best controlled trials.

Can I build muscle while doing intermittent fasting?

Yes, if you eat enough protein and calories within your window and train well. Very short eating windows make it harder.

How Many Days a Week Should You Work Out?

The right number is the one you can stick to. Here's what each option gets you, and example plans.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • 2 to 4 strength sessions per week work well for most people. Beginners do great with 2 to 3.
  • Training each muscle at least twice per week tends to beat once per week (Schoenfeld et al., 2016).
  • When weekly volume is the same, frequency matters less. Pick the schedule you can keep.
  • Health guidelines: muscle-strengthening on 2+ days and 150 to 300 minutes of moderate cardio per week (WHO, 2020).

What matters more: frequency or volume?

For muscle growth, the total number of hard sets per muscle per week is a key driver (see here). How you spread those sets over the week matters less, as long as each muscle gets trained at least twice.

A review found training a muscle twice per week led to more growth than once per week (Schoenfeld et al., 2016). When total volume is equal, studies comparing two, three or more sessions per muscle show similar results.

So the right number of days mostly depends on your schedule and how long you want each session to be.

Option 1: 2 days per week

Who it's for: beginners, busy people, anyone restarting.

Two full-body sessions per week train every muscle twice. Sessions are longer (about 60 minutes) to fit enough sets. Supersets, where you alternate two exercises for different muscles, help keep it efficient (Iversen et al., 2021).

Option 2: 3 days per week

Who it's for: most beginners and many intermediates.

Three full-body sessions, or an alternating A/B plan, hit every muscle two to three times a week with manageable session lengths. It's the classic sweet spot.

Option 3: 4 days per week

Who it's for: intermediates who want more volume or shorter sessions.

An upper/lower split trains every muscle twice per week and keeps sessions around 45 to 60 minutes.

Option 4: 5 or more days per week

Who it's for: experienced lifters who enjoy training and recover well.

Push/pull/legs or upper/lower plus extra sessions allow high volume and short workouts. For most people, it isn't necessary. Five days per week is also harder to sustain during busy periods.

How to choose

Ask yourself: "How many days can I train in a busy week?" Plan for that number. If you have more time in some weeks, add an optional session. It's much better to plan three and do three than to plan five and do two.

What about rest days?

Muscles need recovery to grow. Avoid training the same muscle hard on consecutive days. Rest days can include walking, light cycling, mobility work or sports. Daily steps help your health and fat loss without hurting recovery.

Frequently asked questions

Is 3 days a week enough to build muscle?

Yes. Three full-body sessions per week are enough to build muscle for beginners and many intermediates, as long as you train hard and progress over time.

Is it bad to work out every day?

Not necessarily, if you vary intensity and muscle groups. But most people get better results and stick with it longer when they include rest days.

How many days a week should I work out to lose weight?

Two to four strength sessions to keep muscle, plus daily movement like walking. Fat loss is driven mainly by your diet; training protects muscle and improves health.

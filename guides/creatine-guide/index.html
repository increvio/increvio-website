Creatine: What It Does, How Much to Take and Is It Safe?

The one supplement with strong evidence behind it. Here's everything you need to know, without the hype.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Creatine monohydrate is one of the most studied supplements. It helps with strength, power and muscle gains when combined with training (Kreider et al., 2017).
  • Take 3 to 5 g per day, every day. Timing doesn't matter much.
  • A loading phase is optional. It just fills your stores faster.
  • For healthy people, creatine is considered safe at recommended doses. Talk to a doctor if you have kidney disease or other conditions.

What creatine does

Creatine is a compound your body makes and also gets from meat and fish. In your muscles, it's stored as phosphocreatine, which helps regenerate ATP, the energy molecule your muscles use for short, intense efforts like a heavy set of squats or a sprint.

Supplementing increases your muscle creatine stores. That lets you do a little more work in hard sets: an extra rep here, a slightly heavier weight there. Over weeks and months, that extra work adds up to more strength and muscle.

According to the International Society of Sports Nutrition, creatine monohydrate is the most effective nutritional supplement currently available for increasing high-intensity exercise capacity and lean body mass during training (Kreider et al., 2017).

How much creatine should you take?

ApproachHowWhen stores are full
Daily dose3 to 5 g per dayAfter about 3 to 4 weeks
Loading (optional)About 0.3 g/kg per day (around 20 g) split into 4 doses for 5 to 7 days, then 3 to 5 g per dayAfter about 1 week

Both get you to the same place. Loading is only faster. Many people skip it because large doses can upset the stomach.

When to take it

Timing matters much less than consistency. Take it with a meal at any time of day. If you train, taking it around your workout is fine, but taking it daily is what counts. Creatine works by building up stores, not by an immediate effect.

Which type?

Creatine monohydrate. It has by far the most research behind it, it's cheap and it works. Other forms (HCl, buffered, ethyl ester) haven't been shown to be better. Micronized monohydrate dissolves more easily but is the same thing.

Is creatine safe?

For healthy adults, creatine monohydrate at recommended doses has a strong safety record in research, including long-term studies (Kreider et al., 2017). Common myths:

  • "It damages your kidneys." Studies in healthy people haven't found harm at recommended doses. It can raise blood creatinine levels slightly, which is a lab marker, not necessarily a sign of kidney damage. If you have kidney disease, talk to your doctor first.
  • "It makes you bloated." You may gain around 1 to 2 kg early on, mostly water stored inside muscle cells. This isn't fat, and it doesn't usually look like bloating.
  • "It causes hair loss." This comes from a single small study that measured a hormone, not hair loss itself. The evidence is weak.
  • "It's a steroid." No. Creatine is a natural compound found in food.

Who benefits most?

  • People doing resistance training or sports with short, intense efforts
  • Vegetarians and vegans, who often have lower creatine stores to begin with
  • Older adults combining it with strength training

What creatine won't do

It won't replace training, protein or sleep. It's a small extra on top of the basics: a good program with progressive overload, enough protein and enough calories.

Frequently asked questions

How much creatine should I take per day?

3 to 5 g of creatine monohydrate per day. Larger people can use the upper end.

Do I need to cycle creatine?

No. There is no evidence that you need breaks. You can take it continuously.

Should women take creatine?

Yes, if they want the benefits. Creatine works similarly for women and men. Doses are the same.

Does creatine make you gain fat?

No. Early weight gain from creatine is mostly water stored in the muscles, not fat.

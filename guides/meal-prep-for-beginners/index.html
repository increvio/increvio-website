Meal Prep for Beginners: A Simple Sunday System

Two hours on Sunday, and your weekday meals are decided. Here's how to do it without eating the same sad chicken all week.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Meal prep removes the daily "what should I eat" decision, which is when most diets go off track.
  • Prep components (protein, carbs, vegetables, sauces), not identical meals, so you can mix them.
  • Run steps in parallel: oven, stove and rice cooker at the same time. Two hours is enough for most weeks.
  • Cooked food keeps about 3 to 4 days in the fridge. Freeze the rest or prep twice a week.

Why meal prep works

Healthy eating usually fails on weekday evenings: you're tired, hungry and there's nothing ready. Takeout wins. Meal prep moves the decision and the work to a calm moment on the weekend.

Planning meals is linked to better diet quality. In a study of more than 40,000 adults, people who planned their meals ate a more varied, healthier diet and were less likely to have obesity (Ducrot et al., 2017).

Step 1: Plan your week (15 minutes)

Decide how many meals to prep. Most beginners start with lunches for workdays and maybe a few dinners. Breakfast is often simple enough to make fresh.

Pick:

  • 2 proteins, e.g. chicken thighs and lentils or tofu
  • 2 carb sources, e.g. rice and roasted potatoes
  • 2 to 3 vegetables, e.g. broccoli, peppers, carrots
  • 2 sauces, e.g. yogurt-herb sauce and a peanut or tomato sauce

That's enough to make different combinations every day.

Step 2: Shop with a list

Write a grocery list sorted by store section: produce, meat and fish, dairy, dry goods, frozen. You'll be faster and buy fewer extras. See Weekly meal plan and grocery list for a template.

Step 3: Cook in parallel (about 2 hours)

The trick is to use all your equipment at the same time. A typical order:

TimeOvenStoveCounter
0:00PreheatStart rice or grainsWash and chop vegetables
0:15Potatoes and vegetables inLentils or beansSeason the chicken
0:30Chicken in (second tray)Mix sauces
1:00Everything out, cool downCook eggs for snacksPortion into containers
1:30Clean up, label containers

Let food cool before closing containers and refrigerating it. Get it into the fridge within about two hours of cooking.

Step 4: Store it safely

  • Fridge: cooked meals keep about 3 to 4 days. Prep Sunday for Monday to Wednesday, and either freeze Thursday and Friday portions or do a short second prep midweek.
  • Freezer: most cooked grains, stews, curries and meat keep for months. Raw salads and cooked eggs don't freeze well.
  • Reheat until steaming hot all the way through.

Avoid getting bored

  • Change sauces and spices. The same chicken tastes completely different with curry, pesto or a Mexican spice mix.
  • Mix components daily. Chicken plus rice on Monday, lentils plus potatoes on Tuesday.
  • Rotate recipes weekly. Keep a list of 8 to 10 dishes you like and rotate them.
  • Keep one flexible meal. Leave one evening for cooking fresh or eating out.

Frequently asked questions

How long does meal prep last in the fridge?

Most cooked meals keep about 3 to 4 days in the fridge. Freeze portions you'll eat later in the week, or prep twice per week.

Is meal prep cheaper?

Usually yes. Buying in larger amounts, wasting less food and eating out less often saves money for most people.

Do I have to eat the same thing every day?

No. Prep components and combine them differently, change sauces and spices, and rotate recipes every week.

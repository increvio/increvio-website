Eating Out on a Diet: How to Enjoy Restaurants Without Losing Progress

One restaurant meal never ruins your progress. Here's how to enjoy it and get straight back on track.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Restaurant meals are often large. In one US study, frequently ordered restaurant meals averaged about 1,200 kcal (Urban et al., 2016).
  • Order around a protein source, add vegetables and pick the one treat you really want.
  • Adjust the rest of your day or week a little, but don't skip meals to "save up."
  • The real risk is the "day is ruined" mindset afterwards. Go back to your plan at the next meal.

Why restaurants are tricky

Restaurant food is designed to taste great, which usually means more oil, butter, salt and bigger portions than you'd make at home. In a US study of frequently ordered meals from restaurants in three cities, the average meal contained about 1,200 kcal, roughly half to two thirds of many people's daily needs (Urban et al., 2016).

That doesn't mean you need to avoid restaurants. It means a little planning helps.

Before you go

  • Look at the menu online and decide before you're hungry and surrounded by smells.
  • Eat normally earlier in the day. Arriving starving leads to the bread basket, two drinks and a dessert.
  • Plan lighter meals around it. A slightly smaller lunch and a protein-rich breakfast leave room.

What to order

Build your meal around protein. Steak, chicken, fish, seafood, tofu or legume dishes keep you full and support your goals.

Add vegetables. Ask for a side salad or vegetables instead of (or in addition to) fries.

Pick one treat. Want dessert? Skip the bread. Want a beer or wine? Skip the dessert. You don't have to skip everything, just choose.

Watch the hidden extras. Creamy sauces, dressings, fried toppings and sugary drinks add a lot. Ask for dressings on the side.

After the meal: what to do

Don't compensate by starving. Skipping meals the next day often leads to overeating later. Just return to your normal plan.

Adjust lightly. If you're tracking, you can trim carbs and fat a little over the next one or two days. Keep protein high.

Don't panic about the scale. Salty restaurant food makes you hold water. The scale might jump by a kilo or more the next day. That's water, not fat. It goes away within a few days.

Never miss twice. One restaurant meal is a blip. A "ruined" weekend is a setback. See Never miss twice.

Alcohol

Alcohol has about 7 kcal per gram, and drinks add up quickly. A pint of beer is roughly 200 to 250 kcal, a glass of wine about 120 to 180 kcal. Alcohol also tends to lower your guard around food. If you drink, choose one or two drinks, alternate with water and plan them into your day.

Frequently asked questions

Can I eat out and still lose weight?

Yes. Plan ahead, choose protein-rich dishes with vegetables, pick one treat and return to your normal plan at the next meal. One meal doesn't decide your progress.

What is the healthiest thing to order at a restaurant?

A dish with a clear lean protein source and vegetables, like grilled fish or chicken with a salad, cooked without heavy sauces. Ask for dressings and sauces on the side.

Should I save calories for a big dinner?

A little, yes: lighter lunch, protein-rich breakfast. Don't skip all meals beforehand though. Arriving very hungry usually leads to eating much more.

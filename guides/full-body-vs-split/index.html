Full Body vs Split Workout: Which Is Better for You?

The best split is the one that fits your week and hits each muscle at least twice. Here's how to pick.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Training each muscle at least twice per week tends to beat once per week (Schoenfeld et al., 2016).
  • When weekly volume is matched, full body and split routines build similar muscle (Schoenfeld et al., 2019).
  • 2 to 3 days per week: full body. 4 days: upper/lower. 5 to 6 days: push/pull/legs or similar.
  • Pick based on your schedule, recovery and what you enjoy, not on what's "optimal".

What research says

A meta-analysis found that training a muscle twice per week led to more growth than once per week (Schoenfeld et al., 2016). A later analysis that matched weekly volume found that frequency itself mattered little: what counts is total hard sets per week (Schoenfeld et al., 2019).

So:

  • A classic five-day "chest day, back day, leg day" split works, but it trains each muscle only once and packs a lot of volume into one session.
  • Full body and upper/lower naturally hit each muscle twice or more, which makes it easy to spread volume.

Full body: pros and cons

Pros: each muscle trained often, missing a day matters less, great for beginners and busy people.

Cons: sessions can get long if you need a lot of volume, and leg exercises late in a session can feel tough.

Upper/lower: pros and cons

Pros: shorter sessions, each muscle twice a week, easy to schedule (Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri).

Cons: needs four days; missing one day leaves a body half untrained that week.

Push/pull/legs: pros and cons

Pros: focused sessions, high volume per muscle, popular with intermediates training 5 to 6 days.

Cons: with 3 days, each muscle is trained only once a week; with 6 days, it's a big time commitment.

How to choose

If you...Choose
Are a beginnerFull body, 2 to 3 days
Have a busy, unpredictable weekFull body
Can reliably train 4 daysUpper/lower
Love training and recover well, 5 to 6 daysPPL or upper/lower plus extras
Want to focus one area (e.g. glutes and legs)Any split, with more sets for that area

Example weeks

Full body (3 days): Mon A · Wed B · Fri A, then alternate.

Upper/lower (4 days): Mon upper · Tue lower · Thu upper · Fri lower.

PPL (6 days): push · pull · legs · push · pull · legs · rest.

What matters more than the split

  • Enough hard sets per muscle per week (how many)
  • Progressive overload
  • Showing up consistently for months

A "perfect" split you skip half the time loses to a simple full body plan you never miss.

Frequently asked questions

Is full body or split better for muscle growth?

When weekly volume is equal, both work similarly. Full body makes it easier to train each muscle often; splits allow more volume per session.

Can beginners do a split?

Yes, but full body is usually easier to stick with and trains every muscle more often, which suits beginners well.

Is a bro split bad?

Not bad, just less efficient for most people because each muscle is trained only once per week.

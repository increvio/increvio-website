How Close to Failure Should You Train to Build Muscle?

You don't have to destroy every set. Here's how hard is hard enough, and how to judge it.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Training to complete failure doesn't seem to build meaningfully more muscle than stopping a rep or two short (Refalo et al., 2023).
  • Stopping very far from failure (many reps left) builds less muscle. Sets need to be hard.
  • Aim for 1 to 2 reps in reserve on compound lifts, 0 to 1 on isolation exercises.
  • Less fatigue means better performance in later sets and sessions, and training that feels better.

What is "failure"?

Failure is when you can't complete another rep with good form, no matter how hard you try. Reps in reserve (RIR) describes how close you got: 2 RIR means you could have done two more reps.

What the research says

A 2023 meta-analysis compared training to failure with stopping short of failure (Refalo et al., 2023). When volume was similar, muscle growth was about the same in both. Going all the way to failure didn't add a clear benefit for most people.

A 2024 analysis looked more closely at how proximity to failure relates to results (Robinson et al., 2024). Muscle growth tended to increase the closer people trained to failure, while strength gains didn't depend much on it. In other words: sets should be hard, but the last rep before failure isn't magic.

Together, this suggests a practical middle ground: train close to failure, but you don't have to hit it.

Why not always go to failure?

  • More fatigue. Failure sets take longer to recover from and can reduce performance in later sets.
  • Higher technique risk. Form tends to break down on the very last reps of heavy compound lifts.
  • It feels worse. How you feel during training predicts whether you'll keep doing it (Rhodes & Kates, 2015).

Practical guidelines

Exercise typeRecommended reps in reserve
Heavy compound lifts (squat, deadlift, bench)1 to 3
Machines and dumbbell compound lifts1 to 2
Isolation exercises (curls, raises, extensions)0 to 1
Last set of an exerciseCan go closer to failure

Beginners can stay at 2 to 3 reps in reserve while learning technique.

How to judge reps in reserve

Most people underestimate how many reps they have left, especially beginners. Ways to calibrate:

  • Test it occasionally. On a safe exercise like a machine or dumbbell curl, take a set to failure and compare with your estimate.
  • Watch the bar speed. When reps slow down noticeably, you're usually within 2 to 3 reps of failure.
  • Rate the exercise. After each exercise, rate it as easy, just right or hard. If it's "easy" several times, increase the weight.

How to practice judging reps in reserve

  1. Pick a safe exercise (machine press, leg press, cable row, dumbbell curl).
  2. Before the set, decide: "I'll stop with 2 reps left."
  3. Do the set and stop.
  4. Then immediately continue to failure and count how many extra reps you got.
  5. If you got 5 extra reps, you were further from failure than you thought.

Do this once every few weeks, and your estimates will get much better.

Signs you're training too far from failure

  • Your last rep is as fast as your first
  • You could easily have a conversation during the set
  • You never need to concentrate on the last reps
  • Weights haven't gone up in months even though you feel fine

Signs you're training too close to failure too often

  • Form breaks down on most sets
  • Performance drops a lot from the first to the last set
  • You feel wrecked for days after each session
  • Joints are constantly achy

Proximity to failure by experience

LevelTypical approach
Beginner2 to 3 reps in reserve while learning
Intermediate1 to 2 reps in reserve, isolation 0 to 1
AdvancedVaries by phase; occasional failure sets on safe exercises

Frequently asked questions

Do you need to train to failure to build muscle?

No. Stopping one or two reps short builds about as much muscle with less fatigue. Sets do need to be challenging, though.

Is training to failure dangerous?

On machines and isolation exercises, it's generally safe. On heavy free-weight lifts like squats or bench press, going to failure without a spotter or safety bars increases risk.

How many reps in reserve for beginners?

Around 2 to 3 reps in reserve is a good start while learning technique. Move closer to failure as you get more confident.

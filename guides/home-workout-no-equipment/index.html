Home Workout Without Equipment: Build Muscle With Bodyweight

No gym, no problem. Your muscles respond to effort, not to the price of the equipment.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Muscles grow across a wide range of loads, from heavy to light, as long as sets are taken close to failure (Schoenfeld et al., 2017).
  • Push-ups built similar chest strength and size to bench press in one study when effort was matched (Kikuchi & Nakazato, 2017).
  • Progress by harder variations, more reps, slower tempo and less rest.
  • The best first buy: a pair of adjustable dumbbells or resistance bands, plus a doorframe pull-up bar.

Can you build muscle with bodyweight?

Yes. A meta-analysis found that low-load training builds similar muscle to heavy training when sets are taken close to failure (Schoenfeld et al., 2017). A study comparing push-ups to bench press with similar effort found similar gains in chest muscle thickness and strength (Kikuchi & Nakazato, 2017).

The catch: with light loads, sets need to be hard. Twenty easy push-ups when you could do 40 won't do much. Twenty push-ups when you can do 22 will.

A 3-day home plan

Do on three non-consecutive days. Rest about 60 to 90 seconds between sets. Take each set to about 1 to 3 reps short of failure.

Day A

  1. Bulgarian split squat: 3 sets per leg
  2. Push-up variation: 3 sets
  3. Table row: 3 sets
  4. Single-leg glute bridge: 3 sets per leg
  5. Plank: 2 × 30 to 60 s

Day B

  1. Squat (slow, 3 seconds down): 3 sets
  2. Pike push-up: 3 sets
  3. Towel or table row: 3 sets
  4. Single-leg hip thrust: 3 sets per leg
  5. Side plank: 2 × 30 s per side

How to progress without weights

  1. More reps in the same range (e.g. up to 20 to 25)
  2. Harder variation (incline → standard → feet elevated push-up)
  3. Slower lowering (3 to 4 seconds down)
  4. Pauses at the hardest point
  5. One side at a time (single-leg squats and bridges)
  6. Shorter rest, once reps are high

Write down reps and variations every session so you can beat last time. See Progressive overload.

What to buy first

  1. Resistance bands: cheap, versatile, great for rows and pull-aparts
  2. Doorframe pull-up bar: the best back builder at home
  3. Adjustable dumbbells: open up almost every gym exercise
  4. A sturdy bench or step: for split squats, hip thrusts, presses

Home training and consistency

Home workouts remove the commute, which removes a big excuse. They're also the perfect plan B for busy weeks. Many people mix both: gym on most days, home on chaotic days. See How to work out with a busy schedule.

Frequently asked questions

Can you build muscle with only push-ups and squats?

Yes, especially as a beginner, if sets are hard and you progress with harder variations and more reps. Adding a pulling exercise (rows or pull-ups) is important for balance.

How many reps should I do with bodyweight exercises?

Anything from about 6 to 30 reps works if sets are close to failure. If you can do more than 30, switch to a harder variation.

How often should I do home workouts?

Two to four times per week, with rest days in between for the same muscles.

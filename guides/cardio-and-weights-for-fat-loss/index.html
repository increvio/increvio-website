Cardio or Weights for Fat Loss? Why You Need Both

The fat loss question has a simple answer: diet first, keep lifting, add cardio you enjoy.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Your diet drives fat loss. Exercise helps, but you can't out-train a large calorie surplus.
  • Strength training protects muscle while you lose fat, so you end up leaner, not just smaller.
  • Cardio adds calorie burn and big health benefits. Moderate amounts don't hurt muscle growth (Schumann et al., 2022).
  • A good combination: 2 to 4 strength sessions plus about 150 minutes of moderate cardio per week, and daily steps.

Why diet comes first

Fat loss happens when you consistently eat fewer calories than you burn. A 45-minute cardio session might burn 300 to 500 kcal, which is easy to eat back with one snack. Exercise matters, but a sensible calorie deficit is the main driver. See How fast should you lose weight?

Why you should keep lifting weights

When you eat less, your body can break down muscle along with fat. Strength training sends a clear signal to keep it. Combined with enough protein, it helps make sure the weight you lose is mostly fat (Helms et al., 2014).

That's the difference between looking "skinny" after a diet and looking fit. Same weight, different body composition.

Why cardio helps

  • More calories burned, which makes the deficit easier without eating even less.
  • Heart and lung health. Aerobic fitness is strongly linked to long life.
  • Better recovery and work capacity between sets.
  • Mood and stress benefits.

The WHO recommends 150 to 300 minutes of moderate (or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous) aerobic activity per week for adults, plus strength training on two or more days (WHO, 2020).

Does cardio kill your gains?

This old worry is mostly unfounded. A 2022 meta-analysis found that combining endurance and strength training didn't compromise muscle growth or maximal strength compared to strength training alone. Only explosive power was slightly affected, mainly when both were done in the same session (Schumann et al., 2022).

To minimize any interference:

  • Separate cardio and lifting by a few hours or do them on different days.
  • Prefer low-impact cardio like cycling, walking or rowing on leg-training weeks.
  • Don't add huge amounts of high-intensity cardio suddenly.

Which cardio is best?

The one you'll do. Options:

Zone 2: what it means

"Zone 2" is a moderate intensity where you can still talk in full sentences, but you're breathing noticeably harder. For most people it's a brisk walk uphill, an easy bike ride or a relaxed jog. It's easy to recover from, burns calories and builds aerobic fitness.

Common mistakes

  • Replacing strength training with cardio. You'll lose more muscle along with fat.
  • Doing very hard cardio every day. Hurts recovery and increases hunger.
  • Eating back all exercise calories. Fitness trackers often overestimate burn.
  • Ignoring daily steps. Often the easiest win. See How many steps a day.

Frequently asked questions

Is cardio or weights better for losing belly fat?

You can't choose where you lose fat. Both help with overall fat loss when combined with a calorie deficit. Weights help you keep muscle, so you look leaner when the fat comes off.

Should I do cardio before or after weights?

If building strength and muscle is your priority, lift first or do cardio on separate days. For light cardio like walking, it doesn't matter much.

How much cardio should I do to lose weight?

Around 150 minutes of moderate cardio per week plus daily steps is a good target. More can help, but your diet remains the most important factor.

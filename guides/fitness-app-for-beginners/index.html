Fitness App for Beginners: What to Look For Before You Start

The best beginner app makes the first weeks easy enough that you're still training in month three.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Beginners don't need complex programs. They need a simple plan, clear exercise demos and a start at their level.
  • The biggest risk for beginners isn't a bad program, it's quitting in the first weeks.
  • Look for: onboarding that asks about your level and schedule, 2 to 3 full-body sessions, filled-in weights, rest timer, realistic goals and accountability.
  • Disclosure: written by the team behind Increvio.

What beginners actually need

When you're new, almost any sensible program works. Two or three full-body sessions per week with progressive overload will build strength and muscle (Schoenfeld et al., 2016). What decides your results is whether you're still doing it in three months.

So a beginner app should be judged on one question: does it make the first 8 to 12 weeks easy to stick with?

1. It starts at your level

The first sessions should feel doable, not brutal. Very sore muscles in week one are a common reason people stop (about soreness).

Look for: questions about your experience, training place and equipment, and a first workout you can finish.

2. It shows you how to do each exercise

Not knowing the movements is one of the main sources of gym anxiety.

Look for: a demo for every exercise, simple cues, and easier alternatives.

3. It plans the whole week

Look for: fixed training days, a full-body plan for 2 to 3 days, and the right workout on the right day.

4. It does the math for you

Look for: sets, reps and weights filled in, your numbers from last time, and a clear signal when to go heavier (progressive overload).

5. It sets realistic expectations

Promises like "shredded in 30 days" lead to disappointment. Visible change usually takes a few months.

Look for: realistic timelines and progress you can see early, like strength and consistency.

6. It keeps you accountable

Look for: reminders, a streak with forgiveness, and friends who see that you trained.

7. It covers food without overwhelming you

You don't need to count every calorie from day one. A simple meal plan or a few protein targets go a long way.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best fitness app for beginners?

One that starts at your level, explains every exercise, plans simple full-body workouts, fills in your weights and keeps you accountable. Use the checklist to compare.

Should beginners use a fitness app or a personal trainer?

Both can work. A trainer helps with technique and motivation but costs more. An app with good demos and accountability is a great start, and one or two sessions with a trainer can complement it.

How often should a beginner work out?

Two to three full-body sessions per week is ideal for most beginners. See How many days a week to work out.

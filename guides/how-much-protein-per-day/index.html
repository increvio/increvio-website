How Much Protein Per Day? The Research-Backed Answer

Clear numbers from the research, how to spread protein over the day, and what it looks like on a plate.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • For people who lift weights, about 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight per day captures most of the benefit for muscle. Going up to 2.2 g/kg is a reasonable upper target (Morton et al., 2018).
  • In a calorie deficit, aim for the higher end, since protein helps protect muscle (Helms et al., 2014).
  • Spread protein over 3 to 5 meals with roughly 0.4 to 0.55 g/kg each (Schoenfeld & Aragon, 2018).
  • The official minimum of 0.8 g/kg prevents deficiency. It's not optimal for building muscle.

What the research says

The best summary comes from a 2018 meta-analysis that combined 49 studies with 1,863 participants doing resistance training (Morton et al., 2018). Protein intake beyond about 1.6 g/kg per day didn't add further gains in muscle on average. Because people differ, the authors suggested that going up to about 2.2 g/kg covers almost everyone.

In a calorie deficit, needs go up. A review on lean athletes dieting recommended 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of lean body mass, because the body is more likely to break down muscle when energy is low (Helms et al., 2014). For most people that works out to around 1.8 to 2.2 g per kg of total body weight.

If you have a lot of body fat to lose, calculate with your goal weight or a healthy weight instead of your current weight. Otherwise the numbers get unrealistically high, since fat tissue doesn't need protein.

How to spread protein over the day

Your body uses protein for muscle building best when it arrives in several servings. A review suggested aiming for about 0.4 g/kg per meal across at least four meals to reach 1.6 g/kg, and up to 0.55 g/kg per meal to reach 2.2 g/kg (Schoenfeld & Aragon, 2018).

For a 75 kg person, that's about 30 to 40 g of protein per meal, four times a day.

A protein-rich meal before bed can also help. Studies found that 30 to 40 g of slowly digested protein, like casein from quark or cottage cheese, before sleep supports overnight muscle building (Snijders et al., 2019).

Plant proteins work too. Combine sources (legumes, grains, soy) and aim for the upper end of the range, since some plant proteins contain less leucine per gram.

Is a lot of protein dangerous?

For healthy people, intakes up to around 2.2 g/kg are considered safe in research. People with kidney disease or other health conditions should talk to a doctor before increasing protein.

Do I need protein shakes?

No. Shakes are just a convenient way to reach your target. If you hit your daily protein with regular food, a shake adds nothing extra. If you struggle to eat enough, a shake after training or as a snack is an easy fix.

Frequently asked questions

How much protein do I need to build muscle?

About 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight per day, combined with resistance training. Most of the benefit comes by about 1.6 g/kg.

How much protein do I need to lose weight?

In a calorie deficit, aim for about 1.8 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight, or calculate from your goal weight if you have a lot to lose. Protein keeps you full and protects muscle.

Can the body only absorb 30 g of protein per meal?

No, the body absorbs all of it. Muscle building per meal may level off at moderate amounts, which is why spreading protein over three to five meals is a good strategy, but larger meals aren't wasted.

Do women need less protein?

Relative to body weight, recommendations are similar for women and men. Women usually weigh less, so the total grams are lower.

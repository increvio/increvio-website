How Many Sets Per Week to Build Muscle? The Research Answer

Training volume is one of the biggest drivers of muscle growth. Here's how much you need, and how much is too much.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • 10 or more hard sets per muscle per week led to more growth than fewer than five in a meta-analysis (Schoenfeld et al., 2017).
  • Each extra weekly set added a little more growth on average, with diminishing returns at higher volumes.
  • A "hard set" is a set taken within about 0 to 3 reps of failure. Warm-up sets don't count.
  • Beginners grow well with 6 to 10 sets per muscle. Start lower and add sets over time.

What is training volume?

Training volume is how much work you do. For muscle growth, the simplest useful measure is the number of hard sets per muscle group per week. A hard set is one where you stop close to failure, roughly 0 to 3 reps short of not being able to do another rep with good form.

Weight and reps matter too, but within a broad rep range (around 6 to 30 reps), muscle growth is similar as long as sets are hard (Schoenfeld et al., 2017, JSCR). So counting hard sets works well.

What the research says

A 2017 meta-analysis pooled studies on training volume and muscle growth (Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger, 2017). They found a dose-response relationship: more weekly sets led to more growth. Each additional set per week was associated with an average increase in muscle growth of about 0.37%.

The difference between fewer than 5 and 10 or more sets was clear. Later research suggests gains keep coming at higher volumes but with smaller returns, and very high volumes add fatigue and time.

How many sets should you do?

LevelHard sets per muscle per week
Beginner (first year)6 to 10
Intermediate10 to 16
Advanced12 to 20+, depending on recovery

Start at the lower end and only add sets if progress stalls and you recover well. More isn't always better: if your lifts are going down, you're sore for days and sleep worse, you're probably doing too much.

Priority muscles

You don't need the same volume for every muscle. If you want to focus on one area, like glutes and legs or upper body, give those 12 to 18 sets and keep others at around 8 to 10. Fewer sets still maintain and slowly build muscle.

A note on shoulders: pressing exercises mostly train the front of the shoulder. If you want wider-looking shoulders, add direct work for the side delts, like lateral raises, for about 6 to 8 sets per week.

How to count sets

  • A set of bench press counts as one set for chest. It also trains triceps and front shoulders, which many people count as half a set.
  • Warm-up sets don't count.
  • Sets far from failure (5+ reps left) count much less.

Split your volume across the week

Spreading volume over at least two sessions per muscle per week tends to work better than one huge session. A review found training each muscle twice a week led to more growth than once a week (Schoenfeld et al., 2016). It also keeps sessions shorter and quality higher. More than 8 to 10 hard sets for one muscle in a single session tends to be less productive.

Example for 12 weekly chest sets: 6 sets on Monday, 6 sets on Thursday.

How to add volume over time

  1. Start at the lower end for the first 6 to 8 weeks.
  2. If a muscle stops progressing and you recover well, add 2 sets per week.
  3. Watch for signs of too much: lifts dropping, joints aching, constant fatigue.
  4. Every few months, take a lighter week (deload).

Frequently asked questions

Is 3 sets per exercise enough?

It depends on how many exercises per muscle you do per week. Three sets of one chest exercise twice a week equals six weekly sets, which is fine for beginners. Intermediates usually need more.

Can you do too many sets?

Yes. Beyond your recovery capacity, more sets lead to fatigue, worse performance and possibly less growth. Increase volume gradually and watch your progress.

Do sets to failure count more?

Sets close to failure (0 to 3 reps left) all count as hard sets. Going all the way to failure adds fatigue and only small extra benefit for most people.

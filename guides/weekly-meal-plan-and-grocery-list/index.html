How to Make a Weekly Meal Plan and Grocery List (Template)

Twenty minutes on the weekend saves you from dozens of food decisions during the week.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Plan your meals before you shop, then build the grocery list from the plan.
  • Use a template: same breakfasts, rotating lunches, and a few dinner "slots" with flexible recipes.
  • Sort your grocery list by store section to shop faster and buy fewer extras.
  • People who plan meals tend to eat more varied, healthier food (Ducrot et al., 2017).

Why plan your meals at all?

Without a plan, you decide what to eat when you're hungry, tired and short on time. Those are the moments when takeout and snacks win. A plan moves the decision to a calm moment and makes the healthy choice the default.

It also saves money and food. You buy what you need, use leftovers on purpose and throw away less.

Step 1: Start with your schedule

Look at your week first. Which evenings are busy? Which days do you train? Are there dinners out?

  • Busy days need quick or prepped meals.
  • Training days can include more carbs around your workout.
  • Days with events need fewer planned meals.

Step 2: Use a simple template

MealStrategyExample
Breakfast2 options you rotateOvernight oats, eggs on toast

Dinner4 to 5 recipes + leftoversSalmon bowl, chili, stir fry
Flexible1 to 2 free mealsEating out, cooking something new

Fixed breakfasts and snacks cut decisions in half. Dinners give you variety.

Step 3: Check protein and balance

For each main meal, check:

  • A protein source (meat, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, tofu): about 30 to 40 g for most adults (why)
  • Vegetables or fruit: at least half the plate at lunch and dinner
  • A carb source that fits your goal: rice, potatoes, pasta, bread, oats
  • Some fat: olive oil, nuts, avocado, cheese

Step 4: Write the grocery list by section

Go through each recipe and write the ingredients into store sections. Combine amounts:

  • Produce: broccoli 800 g, peppers 4, bananas 6, berries 2 packs
  • Meat & fish: chicken thighs 1 kg, salmon 2 fillets
  • Dairy & eggs: skyr 2 kg, eggs 10, feta 200 g
  • Dry goods: rice 1 kg, oats 500 g, canned tomatoes 2
  • Frozen: mixed vegetables, berries

Check what you already have at home before you go.

Step 5: Shop and prep

Shop once, maybe with a small top-up midweek for fresh produce. Then prep what you can on the weekend. See Meal prep for beginners.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start meal planning?

Pick a planning day, check your schedule, use a template with fixed breakfasts and snacks, choose four to five dinners, and write a grocery list from those recipes.

How can I make meal planning cheaper?

Use seasonal produce, buy staples in bulk, plan for leftovers, cook with frozen vegetables and use the same ingredients in more than one recipe.

Should I plan every single meal?

No. Leave one or two flexible meals per week for eating out or cooking spontaneously. A plan that leaves room for life lasts longer.

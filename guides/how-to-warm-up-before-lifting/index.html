How to Warm Up Before Lifting Weights (5-Minute Routine)

A good warm-up takes 5 to 10 minutes and makes your first working set feel better. Here's the routine.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good warm-up has three parts: 3 to 5 minutes of light cardio, a few dynamic movements, and ramp-up sets of your first exercise.
  • Warm-ups can improve performance and may reduce injury risk (McCrary et al., 2015).
  • Short static stretches (under about 60 seconds per muscle) have little negative effect, but long static stretching right before lifting isn't needed (Behm et al., 2016).
  • Later exercises need fewer warm-up sets because you're already warm.

Why warm up?

Warming up raises muscle temperature, increases blood flow and gets your nervous system ready to produce force. It also lets you rehearse the movement with light weights, so your first heavy set feels smooth instead of stiff. A review of upper-body warm-ups found evidence that they improve performance, with some evidence for lower injury risk (McCrary et al., 2015).

The 5 to 10 minute routine

Part 1: General warm-up (3 to 5 minutes)

Anything that gets you slightly warm and breathing a bit harder:

  • Bike, rower, cross-trainer or brisk incline walk
  • At home: jumping jacks, marching, light bodyweight squats

Part 2: Dynamic movements (2 to 3 minutes)

Move through the ranges you'll use in the workout. 8 to 10 reps each:

Lower body dayUpper body day
Bodyweight squatsArm circles
Leg swings (front and side)Band pull-aparts
Hip hinges / good mornings without weightPush-ups (easy)
Walking lungesScapular push-ups
Glute bridgesLight band rows

Part 3: Ramp-up sets (3 to 5 minutes)

Before your first heavy exercise, do 2 to 4 lighter sets, increasing the weight and lowering the reps:

Ramp-up setWeight (of working weight)Reps
1Empty bar or about 40%8 to 10
2About 60%5
3About 80%2 to 3
Working sets100%As planned

For later exercises that use similar muscles, one light set is usually enough.

What about stretching?

Long static stretches (holding for 60 seconds or more per muscle) right before lifting can slightly reduce strength for a short time. Shorter stretches, especially as part of a full warm-up, have little negative effect (Behm et al., 2016). If you like stretching or need it for mobility, keep holds short before training, or stretch after.

Morning workouts

Your body feels stiffer in the morning. Add a couple of extra minutes of general warm-up and an extra ramp-up set. See Morning vs evening workout.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the warm-up and jumping straight into heavy sets
  • Doing a 20-minute warm-up that tires you out
  • Warming up with exercises unrelated to the workout
  • Long static stretching right before heavy squats or presses

Frequently asked questions

How long should a warm-up be before lifting?

Five to ten minutes is enough for most people: a few minutes of light cardio, a few dynamic movements and two to four ramp-up sets.

Do I need to stretch before lifting?

Not necessarily. Dynamic movements and ramp-up sets prepare you better. Short stretches are fine; long static holds right before heavy lifting aren't needed.

Should I warm up for every exercise?

Do full ramp-up sets for your first big lift, and one quick lighter set for later exercises that use different movements.

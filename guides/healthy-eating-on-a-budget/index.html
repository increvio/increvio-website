Healthy Eating on a Budget: High-Protein Meals That Don't Cost Much

Eating for your goals doesn't have to be expensive. Planning beats price tags.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Planning saves the most money: you buy what you need and waste less.
  • The cheapest protein sources are usually eggs, lentils, beans, quark, chicken thighs, canned fish and tofu.
  • Frozen vegetables and fruit are nutritious and cheap; their nutrient levels are often similar to fresh (Li et al., 2017).
  • Cooking in batches turns cheap staples into a week of meals.

Plan first, shop second

Most food money is lost to impulse buys, takeout and food that goes bad. A weekly plan with a grocery list fixes all three. People who plan meals also tend to eat a more varied, healthier diet (Ducrot et al., 2017). See Weekly meal plan and grocery list.

Cheap protein sources

Prices vary by country and store, but these are typically among the best value per gram of protein:

FoodProteinNotes
Eggsabout 6 g per eggVersatile, quick
Dried lentilsabout 25 g per 100 g dryVery cheap, filling, fiber
Canned beans and chickpeasabout 7 g per 100 gNo soaking needed
Low-fat quarkabout 12 g per 100 gOften the cheapest dairy protein
Chicken thighsabout 25 g per 100 g cookedCheaper and juicier than breast
Canned tuna or sardinesabout 25 g per 100 gLong shelf life
Tofuabout 12 to 15 g per 100 gCheap plant protein
Milkabout 3.5 g per 100 mlEasy extra protein
Frozen fish filletsabout 18 to 20 g per 100 gCheaper than fresh

Cheap carbs and vegetables

  • Oats, rice, potatoes, pasta, whole-grain bread: cheap energy for training
  • Frozen vegetables: broccoli, spinach, peas, mixed vegetables. A comparison found frozen produce often has nutrient levels similar to fresh, sometimes higher after a few days of fridge storage (Li et al., 2017).
  • Seasonal produce: carrots, cabbage, onions, apples and bananas are cheap year-round in many places
  • Frozen berries for breakfasts and snacks

A budget grocery list for one week

  • 20 eggs
  • 1 kg low-fat quark
  • 1 kg chicken thighs
  • 500 g dried lentils
  • 2 cans chickpeas, 2 cans tuna
  • 1 kg rice, 1 kg oats, 2 kg potatoes
  • 2 kg frozen vegetables, 500 g frozen berries
  • Onions, garlic, carrots, bananas, apples
  • Canned tomatoes, spices, oil

Budget meal ideas

  • Lentil and vegetable curry with rice: big batch, freezes well
  • Chicken thigh tray bake with potatoes and frozen vegetables
  • Egg fried rice with frozen peas and carrots
  • Overnight oats with quark and frozen berries
  • Chickpea and tuna salad with onion and lemon
  • Shakshuka: eggs in a spicy tomato sauce with bread

More money-saving tips

  • Cook once, eat three times. Batch cooking is cheaper per portion. See Meal prep for beginners.
  • Buy store brands for staples.
  • Use the same ingredients in several meals to avoid waste.
  • Check your fridge before shopping.
  • Protein powder can be cheap per gram of protein, but whole foods are more filling.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest source of protein?

Dried lentils, eggs, low-fat quark and chicken thighs are usually among the cheapest. Canned fish and tofu are also good value.

Can you build muscle on a budget?

Yes. Muscle building needs enough calories, enough protein and good training, not expensive foods or supplements.

Are frozen vegetables as healthy as fresh?

Often yes. Frozen produce is picked and frozen at peak ripeness, and studies find similar nutrient levels to fresh produce.

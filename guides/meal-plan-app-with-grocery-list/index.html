Meal Plan App With Grocery List: What to Look For

A good meal plan app doesn't just give you recipes. It removes the daily "what should I eat?" for the whole week.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Meal planning is linked to a more varied, healthier diet and lower obesity rates (Ducrot et al., 2017).
  • For fitness goals, a meal plan app should hit your calories and protein, not just look nice.
  • Must-haves: your preferences and dislikes, cooking time and budget, a grocery list sorted by aisle, and a way to adjust when you eat something else.
  • Disclosure: written by the team behind Increvio. The criteria apply to any app.

Why use a meal planning app?

Planning meals yourself takes time: choosing recipes, checking nutrition, writing a list, adjusting portions. Most people do it for a week or two and stop. An app can do the planning in seconds, which makes it much more likely you'll keep doing it.

1. It hits your targets

For muscle gain or fat loss, the plan should be built around your calories and protein. A good target for active people is about 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight (Morton et al., 2018).

Look for: targets calculated from your body data and goal, protein in every meal, and simple visual feedback (rings or bars) rather than spreadsheets.

2. It fits your taste, time and budget

A plan you don't enjoy is a plan you'll abandon.

Look for: dislikes and allergies, diet styles (vegetarian, no pork, etc.), cooking time per meal, budget options, and enough variety.

3. The grocery list is automatic and sorted

The grocery list should come straight from the plan, with amounts combined across meals and sorted by store section. That alone saves a lot of time and money.

Look for: aisle sorting, check-off while shopping, sharing with your partner or roommates.

4. It helps with meal prep

Cooking for several days at once is the easiest way to stick to a plan on busy weekdays.

Look for: a prep guide that tells you what to cook in which order, ideally with steps running in parallel (oven, stove, counter). See Meal prep for beginners.

5. It adapts when life happens

You'll eat out, skip a meal or have pizza with friends. A good app doesn't mark the day as failed. It adjusts the rest of your day or week.

Look for: quick logging of what you actually ate (text or photo) and automatic replanning.

6. It takes very little daily effort

If you need to log every gram, you'll stop. The best plans need just a tap: "eaten."

Frequently asked questions

What is the best meal planning app with a grocery list?

One that builds plans around your calories, protein, taste, budget and cooking time, generates a sorted grocery list automatically, and adapts when you eat something else.

Are meal planning apps worth it?

For most people who struggle with consistency, yes. Planning ahead is linked to better diet quality, and an app removes most of the effort.

Can a meal plan app help with muscle gain?

Yes, if it plans enough calories and protein for your goal and fits your training schedule.

How Much Water Should You Drink a Day? (Including Workouts)

The 8-glasses rule is close, but the full picture is simpler and more flexible than you might think.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • European guidelines set adequate total water intake at about 2.0 L per day for women and 2.5 L for men, including water from food (EFSA, 2010).
  • About 20 to 30% of that typically comes from food, so drinks make up the rest.
  • You need more when you sweat: training, heat, physical work.
  • Pale yellow urine and rarely feeling thirsty are good practical signs.

The official numbers

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) set "adequate intakes" for total water for adults (EFSA, 2010):

Total water per dayFrom drinks (approx.)
Women2.0 Labout 1.5 to 1.6 L
Men2.5 Labout 1.9 to 2.0 L

"Total water" includes water from food. Fruit, vegetables, soups, yogurt and cooked grains contain a lot of it. That's why the drinking target is lower than the total.

So the classic "8 glasses a day" (about 2 L) is a reasonable rule of thumb for many people, but not a strict requirement.

When you need more

  • Training: you can lose 0.5 to 1.5 L of sweat per hour, more in heat.
  • Hot weather or warm rooms
  • Physical jobs
  • High-protein or high-fiber diets (fiber needs water to work well)
  • Illness with fever, vomiting or diarrhea

Drinking around workouts

  • Before: drink normally during the day. A glass of water 1 to 2 hours before training is enough for most people.
  • During: for sessions up to about an hour, sip water as you feel thirsty.
  • After: drink to thirst and include salt from your next meal. For very sweaty sessions, about 1.25 to 1.5 L per kg of body weight lost is a common recommendation from sports nutrition research.

For typical gym sessions, plain water is enough. Sports drinks make sense mainly for long, intense or hot sessions.

Does water help with weight loss?

Water has no calories, so replacing sugary drinks with water is one of the easiest ways to cut calories. In one study, older adults on a diet who drank 500 ml of water before each main meal lost more weight over 12 weeks than those who didn't (Dennis et al., 2010). Water before meals may help some people feel fuller.

Signs you're drinking enough

  • Your urine is pale yellow most of the day.
  • You rarely feel very thirsty.
  • No headaches or unusual fatigue in the afternoon.

Dark urine, strong thirst and headaches can be signs you need more.

Can you drink too much?

It's rare, but possible. Drinking very large amounts quickly, especially during long endurance events, can dilute the sodium in your blood. For normal daily life and gym training, drinking to thirst is safe.

Easy ways to drink more

  • Fill a large bottle in the morning and keep it in sight.
  • Drink a glass with every meal.
  • Add lemon, mint, cucumber or a splash of juice.
  • Sparkling water, tea and coffee count too. Coffee in normal amounts still hydrates.
  • Link it to habits: a glass after brushing your teeth, one when you sit down at your desk.

Frequently asked questions

How much water should I drink if I work out?

Your normal daily amount (about 1.5 to 2 L from drinks) plus roughly 0.5 to 1 L per hour of sweaty training. Drink to thirst and watch urine color.

Does coffee count toward water intake?

Yes. In normal amounts, coffee and tea contribute to hydration. Very large amounts of caffeine can increase urine output slightly.

Is 3 liters of water a day too much?

For most healthy adults, 3 L spread over the day is fine, especially if you train or it's hot. Spread it out instead of drinking large amounts at once.

Deload Week: What It Is and When You Need One

Sometimes the fastest way forward is a lighter week. Here's how to deload without losing progress.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A deload is a planned week of easier training: fewer sets and/or more reps in reserve.
  • Purpose: let accumulated fatigue clear so you can keep progressing, and give joints and motivation a break.
  • A common approach: half the sets, stop 3 to 4 reps before failure, keep the same exercises.
  • One week of lighter training doesn't erase gains. Strength is largely maintained for weeks.

Why deload?

Hard training builds fitness and fatigue at the same time. Over many weeks, fatigue can pile up faster than you recover: weights feel heavier, joints ache, sleep gets worse and motivation drops. A deload reduces the training stress for a short time so fatigue can fall while fitness stays.

Many coaches and lifters use deloads, and surveys of strength coaches describe them as a common tool to manage fatigue (Bell et al., 2023). Research on the best structure is still limited, so think of deloads as a practical tool rather than a precise science.

Signs you might need one

  • Lifts stalled or going down for two to three weeks
  • Everything feels heavy, even warm-ups
  • Joints or tendons are achy
  • Poor sleep and low motivation to train
  • You've trained hard for 6 to 8 weeks or more without a break

How to deload

Sets100%about 50%
Effort1 to 2 reps in reserve3 to 4 reps in reserve
WeightNormalSame or slightly lighter
ExercisesSameSame
Training daysSameSame or one fewer

Keep the routine: show up on your normal days, do less. That protects the habit.

When to deload

  • Planned: every 6 to 10 weeks of hard training, especially if you train 4+ days or are intermediate or advanced.
  • Reactive: when you notice the signs above.
  • Beginners often don't need planned deloads in the first months; their sessions are less fatiguing and progress is fast.

Will I lose muscle?

No. One week of reduced training doesn't cause meaningful muscle loss. Strength is typically maintained for at least three weeks without training (Mujika & Padilla, 2000). One study found that a one-week deload in the middle of a training program didn't hurt muscle growth compared to continuous training, though strength gains in some lifts were slightly smaller (Coleman et al., 2024).

Deload vs taking a week off

A full week off works too, especially for vacations or illness. A deload keeps the routine and movement patterns alive, which some people find makes it easier to get back to full training.

Frequently asked questions

How often should you deload?

Every 6 to 10 weeks of hard training is a common guideline, or whenever performance and motivation drop for a couple of weeks.

Should I deload as a beginner?

Usually not on a schedule. Beginners recover quickly. Take a lighter week if you feel run down.

Can I do cardio during a deload?

Yes, light to moderate cardio and walking are fine and can help you feel good.

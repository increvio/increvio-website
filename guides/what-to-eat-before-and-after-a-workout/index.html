What to Eat Before and After a Workout (Simple Guide)

Pre- and post-workout nutrition is simpler than supplement ads suggest. Here's what actually matters.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Total daily protein and calories matter far more than exact timing.
  • Eat a meal with protein and carbs 1 to 3 hours before, or a small snack 30 to 60 minutes before.
  • After training, have a protein-rich meal within a few hours. The "30-minute anabolic window" is much wider than once believed (Aragon & Schoenfeld, 2013).
  • If you train fasted in the morning, eating protein soon after matters a bit more.

Do you need special workout nutrition?

Supplement marketing makes workout nutrition sound complicated. Research says the basics do most of the work: enough calories, about 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg spread over the day, and enough carbs to fuel your training (Kerksick et al., 2017). Timing is the fine-tuning on top.

Before your workout

Goal: have energy, don't feel heavy or hungry.

Time before trainingWhat to eatExamples
2 to 3 hoursA normal meal with protein, carbs, some fatChicken, rice and vegetables; pasta with tuna
1 to 2 hoursA lighter meal or bigger snackYogurt with oats and fruit; a sandwich with turkey
30 to 60 minutesA small, easy-to-digest snackBanana; rice cakes with honey; a small shake

Avoid very fatty or very high-fiber meals right before training if they upset your stomach.

What about training fasted?

Many people train well in the morning without eating. For shorter sessions, it's fine. If you feel weak or dizzy, or you train long and hard, a small snack like a banana usually helps performance. If you train fasted, eat a protein-rich meal soon after.

After your workout

Goal: support muscle repair and refill energy.

  • Protein: about 0.4 to 0.5 g per kg (roughly 25 to 40 g for most adults).
  • Carbs: a normal portion, more if you train again soon or did a long session.
  • Timing: within a few hours. If you ate a meal 1 to 2 hours before training, there's no rush.

The "anabolic window" myth

The idea that you must drink a shake within 30 minutes or lose your gains is outdated. A review concluded that the window is wide: having protein in the hours before and after training (a meal before plus a meal after) covers it for most people (Aragon & Schoenfeld, 2013). If your last meal was long before training, eating soon after matters more.

Post-workout meal ideas

  • Chicken, rice and vegetables
  • Salmon with potatoes and salad
  • Omelet with whole-grain toast
  • Greek yogurt or skyr with oats and berries
  • Lentil curry with rice
  • Protein shake and a banana when you're on the go

Before bed

On training days, a slowly digested protein like quark or cottage cheese before sleep (about 30 to 40 g) can support overnight muscle building (Snijders et al., 2019).

Do you need supplements?

No. A post-workout shake is convenient but not magic. The one supplement with strong evidence for strength and muscle is creatine (guide), and it works regardless of timing.

Frequently asked questions

What should I eat 30 minutes before a workout?

Something small and easy to digest with carbs: a banana, rice cakes with honey, a small yogurt or a piece of toast with jam.

Is it bad to work out on an empty stomach?

Not for most people and most workouts. If performance suffers or you feel unwell, eat a small snack beforehand.

Do I need a protein shake after a workout?

No. A normal meal with protein within a few hours works just as well. Shakes are simply convenient.

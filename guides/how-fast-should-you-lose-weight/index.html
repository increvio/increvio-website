How Fast Should You Lose Weight? A Safe, Realistic Pace

Faster isn't better. Here's the pace that protects your muscle, keeps hunger manageable and actually lasts.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • A sustainable pace for most people is about 0.5 to 1% of body weight per week (Helms et al., 2014).
  • Slower weight loss helped athletes keep or even gain muscle and strength, while faster loss didn't (Garthe et al., 2011).
  • Keep protein high and keep lifting weights. Both protect muscle in a deficit.
  • Expect bumps: water weight makes the scale jump around. Judge progress by the weekly average.

The research on weight loss speed

Fast weight loss sounds attractive, but most of what you want to lose is fat, not muscle or water. The faster you go, the harder it is to protect muscle and the harder it is to stick with the plan.

A study in elite athletes compared two rates of weight loss: about 0.7% of body weight per week versus about 1.4% per week. The slower group increased lean mass and improved strength more, while the faster group did not gain lean mass (Garthe et al., 2011).

Based on studies like this, a review on dieting for physique athletes recommended losing about 0.5 to 1% of body weight per week (Helms et al., 2014). That's a good target for most people.

People with more weight to lose can usually go toward the upper end. People who are already lean should go slower.

How big should your calorie deficit be?

Roughly, 1 kg of body fat stores about 7,700 kcal. Losing 0.5 kg per week needs an average deficit of about 550 kcal per day. In reality, the body adapts a bit as you lose weight, so treat this as a starting point and adjust based on your trend.

Guard rails:

  • Don't go below roughly 1,200 kcal per day for women or 1,500 kcal per day for men without medical supervision.
  • Don't cut more than about 25% below your maintenance calories.
  • If you feel constantly exhausted, dizzy or obsessed with food, slow down.

How to protect your muscle

1. Keep lifting. Strength training tells your body to keep muscle. Don't switch to only cardio. See Cardio and weights for fat loss.

2. Eat enough protein. In a deficit, aim for about 1.8 to 2.2 g/kg of body weight. See How much protein per day.

3. Don't go too fast. As the research shows, a moderate pace protects muscle better.

Why the scale lies

Daily weight can jump by 1 to 2 kg because of salt, carbs, water, digestion and hormones. If you weigh yourself once a week, you might just catch a high day and think nothing works.

Better: weigh yourself several mornings per week, under the same conditions, and look at the weekly average or trend line. Judge your rate of loss over two to three weeks, not days.

Take breaks from dieting

Long, uninterrupted diets get harder over time. Hunger goes up, energy goes down. In a study with men with obesity, alternating two weeks of dieting with two weeks at maintenance led to more fat loss and better long-term results than continuous dieting (Byrne et al., 2018). Planning a few weeks at maintenance after about 12 weeks of dieting is a sensible approach. More in Weight loss plateau.

Frequently asked questions

Is losing 1 kg a week healthy?

For people with a lot of weight to lose, around 1 kg per week can be appropriate at the start. For most people, 0.5 to 1% of body weight per week is a better long-term pace.

Why am I losing weight so fast in the first week?

The first week often includes water and glycogen loss, especially if you eat fewer carbs. After that, the rate slows down to your real fat loss pace.

How long should a diet last?

Many people do well with phases of about 8 to 16 weeks, followed by a few weeks at maintenance. Longer goals can be reached with multiple phases.

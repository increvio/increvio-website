When-Then Plans: The Easiest Way to Actually Do Your Workouts

One sentence, written down, is one of the most effective tools in behavior science. Here's how to use it.

By Timur Leontyev, Founder of Increvio
Updated November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • An implementation intention is a plan in the form: "When situation X happens, I will do Y."
  • In one study, 91% of people who made such a plan exercised, versus around 35 to 38% of the others (Milne et al., 2002).
  • A review of 94 studies found a medium to large effect on reaching goals (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).
  • Add an if-then backup for obstacles: "If it rains, I'll do the home workout."

The experiment

In 2002, psychologists Sarah Milne, Sheina Orbell and Paschal Sheeran split 248 young adults into three groups:

  1. A control group that just read something unrelated.
  2. A motivation group that read about the health benefits of exercise.
  3. A motivation plus plan group that read the same text and then completed one sentence: "During the next week, I will partake in at least 20 minutes of vigorous exercise on [day] at [time of day] at/in [place]."

Over the next week, about 38% of the control group and 35% of the motivation group exercised. In the group that wrote the sentence, 91% did (Milne et al., 2002).

Motivation alone didn't change anything. A single, specific sentence nearly tripled follow-through.

Why it works

When you decide in advance when and where, the situation itself becomes the trigger. You don't have to remember, weigh options or find the energy to decide. "It's Monday at 6 pm and I'm leaving the office" automatically leads to "I'm going to the gym."

Psychologist Peter Gollwitzer describes it as passing control of the behavior to the environment. A review of 94 studies found these plans reliably help people reach goals, from exercise to healthy eating (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).

How to write a good when-then plan

A good plan has three parts: when, where and what.

Weak planStrong plan
I'll work out more.Monday, Wednesday, Friday at 6:15 pm I'll do my workout at the gym next to the office.
I'll eat healthier.When I get home from work, I'll eat the dinner I prepped on Sunday.
I'll walk more.After lunch, I'll walk around the block for 15 minutes.

Tips:

  • Use a cue you can't miss. "After I drop the kids at school" or "when I close my laptop" works better than "in the evening."
  • Be specific about the place. "The gym on Main Street" beats "the gym."
  • Write it down. In the study, people physically wrote the sentence.
  • Put it in your calendar. Treat it like a meeting.

Add if-then plans for obstacles

The second level is planning for what will go wrong. Think about the two or three things that usually stop you, and make a backup:

  • If I have to work late, then I'll do the 20-minute version at home.
  • If it's raining, then I'll do my run on the treadmill.
  • If I'm too tired after work, then I'll just go and do the first two exercises.
  • If friends invite me out for dinner, then I'll pick a protein-rich main and skip the second drink.

These "coping plans" keep one obstacle from cancelling the whole session.

When-then plans for eating

Food decisions happen many times a day, which makes them perfect for implementation intentions:

  • When I do my weekly grocery shopping, I'll buy what's on my meal plan list.
  • When I get hungry in the afternoon, I'll eat the yogurt and fruit I packed.
  • When I eat out, I'll order a dish with a clear protein source.

Combine this with a weekly meal plan and grocery list and most daily food decisions disappear.

Frequently asked questions

What is an implementation intention?

A specific plan in the form "When situation X arises, I will perform behavior Y." It links a goal to a concrete cue, so the behavior starts more automatically.

Do when-then plans work long term?

They're strongest in the first weeks, when habits haven't formed yet. Over time, repeating the plan in the same situation turns it into a habit, which is exactly the goal.

How many plans should I make?

Start with one or two. Too many plans at once can be confusing. One for training days and one for your biggest obstacle is a great start.
